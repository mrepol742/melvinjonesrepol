When I started Webvium in 2018, I had the project name, the idea, and eventually the browser itself, but there was one thing I couldn't get: `webvium.com`. Someone had beaten me to it. At the time, the domain was being used by what appeared to be a Turkey-based web design company, so it wasn't just sitting there waiting for an offer. I accepted it and kept building Webvium anyway. After all, not owning the perfect `.com` wasn't going to stop the project.

Years passed, Webvium continued to exist, and I mostly stopped thinking about the domain. It became one of those things permanently stored in my head as **"taken."**

Turns out, the internet occasionally gives things back.

## One Random Search

Last month, Webvium crossed my mind and I decided to search for `webvium.com` on Namecheap again.

I wasn't trying to acquire it. I wasn't monitoring the expiration date or running some elaborate domain-watching setup. I was just curious.

Then I saw it:

**Available.**

I genuinely had to check twice.

A domain I had known as unavailable for years was suddenly sitting there with an Add to Cart button. I didn't know how long it had been available or how long it would stay that way, and I definitely wasn't interested in finding out.

So I bought it.

No negotiation. No domain broker. No five-figure asking price. Just several years of waiting followed by a surprisingly normal checkout page.

## More Than Just a Better URL

Owning `webvium.com` doesn't change how the browser works, but it changes how the project can present itself.

Webvium now has the most obvious home for its name. Instead of relying on a page under my personal website or another temporary address, the project can stand on its own at a domain people can easily remember.

It also came at an interesting point in Webvium's history.

We are now in the process of filing a trademark application for **Webvium** with the Intellectual Property Office of the Philippines (IPOPHL). The trademark is not registered yet, and there is still a proper application process to go through, but after maintaining the project for years, securing the domain and working toward protecting the name feel like natural next steps.

The funny part is comparing that to 2018.

Back then, I couldn't even own `webvium.com`.

Now the domain belongs to Webvium, the project is still being developed, and we're taking steps toward formally protecting the name too.

## Sometimes You Just Have to Check Again

There isn't some clever domain acquisition strategy behind this story. If anything, the lesson is much simpler.

A domain being taken today doesn't necessarily mean it will be taken forever. Companies close, projects disappear, priorities change, and registrations expire. If there's a domain that perfectly matches something you've been building for years, checking it again occasionally costs almost nothing.

Just don't check it every morning. That's probably when it becomes a problem.

For Webvium, I happened to look again at the right time.

After years of building the project without it, `webvium.com` finally feels like the address Webvium was supposed to have all along. And this time, I'm making sure we keep it.
