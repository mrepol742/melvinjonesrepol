For as long as I can remember using Search Console, it had one core assumption baked into it: you own a domain. Verify a website, get your Performance report, watch your impressions slowly climb, repeat forever. Fine if you're running a blog or a SaaS landing page. Less fine if half your actual audience finds you through a TikTok that showed up in someone's Google search, or an Instagram Reel that Discover decided to surface, and you had zero visibility into any of it. That data existed somewhere in Google's systems. You just weren't allowed to see it unless you had a site to attach it to.

That finally changed on July 7, 2026, when Google rolled out "platform properties," a new Search Console property type that tracks how your Instagram, TikTok, X, and YouTube posts perform in Google Search and Discover, with no website required. It's a small phrase, "no website required," but it quietly undoes years of Search Console being unusable for a huge chunk of the people actually creating content online.

## What You Actually Get

You add each platform as its own separate property, the same way you'd add a domain. Once connected, you get clicks, impressions, and the specific search terms that lead people to your content, filterable down to the individual post. So you can finally answer the question that used to be a total guess: which of your posts is Google actually surfacing, and what is someone typing to find it. There's an Insights report layered on top that surfaces recent traffic trends and your top-performing posts, plus a lighter Achievements report tracking milestones like hitting a new click threshold in the last 28 days.

A few things worth knowing before you go set this up. It works even if you don't have a website at all, which is a genuine first for Search Console, and each account gets tracked as its own separate property, so running the same brand across four platforms means four separate connections, not one unified dashboard. Also worth noting, since the naming has gotten messy: this is not the December 2025 "social channels" experiment and not the June "Search profiles" launch either, those were different things, and LinkedIn isn't in the mix yet.

## Why This Actually Matters

The obvious win is for creators and social-first brands who never had a domain to hang Search Console off of in the first place. But I think the more interesting shift is what it says about how Google itself now treats social content: not as something separate from "search," but as another surface it indexes and ranks alongside regular web pages. If your TikTok is already showing up in Google results whether you like it or not, you might as well be able to see the query data behind it. One social media manager put it well, calling this the biggest social SEO update of the year, and honestly that tracks. It's less a new feature than an overdue acknowledgment of where people are actually searching now.

---

Rollout has been staggered since the announcement, so if it's not in your account yet, give it a bit. It's worth setting up the moment it shows up though, mostly because you've probably had no idea what's been driving your social traffic from Search this whole time.
